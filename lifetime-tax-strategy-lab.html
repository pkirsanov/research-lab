Household declarations

Nothing is assumed. A member you do not declare is named unavailable rather than filled in with a default.

Your Social Security benefit

There are two ways to establish the benefit and you declare exactly ONE of them. Either you read the Primary Insurance Amount off your own Social Security statement, or you give the earnings record and it is computed. Declaring neither, or both, is refused by name rather than resolved by preferring one — they are two different claims about the same figure and neither outranks the other. Nothing here leaves this browser.

Claim ages you want compared

Give the whole-year claim ages you are weighing. They are compared in the order you write them and nothing here is sorted, ranked or picked for you. The comparison multiplies the annual benefit each claim age produces by a whole number of years read from a published table, and reports the age at which two such products are equal. That is arithmetic over your own figures and a published one; it is not a forecast about anyone. The table publishes more than one column of remaining years and you choose which to read — what each column describes is stated in the Power view, including the part of it this retrieval did not establish.

Medicare premiums

Your Medicare premium is set by your income from an EARLIER year, not this one. Give that year's modified adjusted gross income and say which year it is. The year is not worked out for you: the rule pack states which year its premiums read, this page checks the year you give against it, and it refuses rather than quietly using the income you entered above. What comes out is a household cost shown beside the federal tax total and added into no part of it.
