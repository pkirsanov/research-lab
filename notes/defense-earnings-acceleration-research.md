# Defense Earnings Acceleration

Research snapshot as of 2026-08-10. This note supports the
[`defense-earnings-acceleration`](../research/agenda/topics/defense-earnings-acceleration.definition.json)
recurring agenda topic. It records what one dated research session established,
including the correction it made to its own first answer. It is a snapshot, not
a current market read: every figure below is as-of the snapshot date and must be
re-derived by the recurring review before it is used.

## Bottom line

The strongest theme is multi-year stockpile replenishment, and it survives
de-escalation. Air defence, precision missiles, rocket motors, artillery and
propellant carry the clearest funded demand, because inventories consumed in
current conflicts have to be rebuilt after a ceasefire, not during one.

## The correction that matters most

The session's first ranking was wrong in a way worth preserving, because the
same mistake is easy to repeat on every future review.

The first pass ranked companies by **demand exposure** — who is most exposed to
the conflicts generating the demand. That is not what moves a share price. Price
movement decomposes approximately as:

$$\frac{\Delta P}{P}\approx\frac{\Delta EPS_{\text{consensus}}}{EPS_{\text{consensus}}}+\frac{\Delta(P/E)}{P/E}$$

A company can have overwhelming demand exposure and still not re-rate, because
that demand is already in consensus. What moves the price is the **gap between
consensus and what the funded order book will actually deliver** — the estimate
revision still to come. That is why the topic definition now carries a
`revision-gap-ranking` section ahead of the raw capacity read.

The revised method models only capacity likely to convert to revenue by 2027,
applies conservative incremental margins, and **discounts unfunded framework
ceilings** — an announced multi-year framework is not appropriated money.

real and largely priced.

## Funded-program catalysts

These are the named, funded anchors the session used. The recurring review reads
them as catalysts and must re-check appropriation status, because a framework
ceiling is not cash.

- A $58.6 billion PAC-3 framework, described as the largest such award.
- Roughly $1 billion of government investment in solid-rocket-motor capacity.
- The EU SAFE programme, about €150 billion, beginning disbursement and favouring
  European production.
- An additional Taiwan defence package of about $25 billion, weighted to US
  systems.

## Tension outlook

- **Iran** carries the highest immediate demand impact: interceptor and
  precision-strike inventories are depleted, and replenishment is unavoidable
  after any ceasefire.
- **Ukraine** remains attritional as the base case; even a limited air ceasefire
  would not remove the need to rebuild interceptor and artillery stocks.
- **Taiwan** reads as gray-zone pressure and blockade preparation near term
  rather than invasion.
- **Europe** is shifting spend toward domestic production.

## Limits

- Earnings-visibility ratings are not expected stock returns, and the modelled
  price skews are scenario ranges rather than targets. They exclude dividends
  and currency effects.
- The ranking depends on consensus estimates at the snapshot date. Consensus
  moves, so the gap that drove the ranking can close without any change in the
  underlying order book.
- A spin-off or restructuring changes the entity being ranked; the session
  flagged this for L3Harris's missile unit.
- This note is topic research. It is not a recommendation, and the agenda's
  public boundary still applies: no position, size, or holding is recorded here.
