# U.S.-Israel-Iran Cross-Asset Equity Screen

Research snapshot as of 2026-08-19. This companion expands the 15-by-15 screen
in [the conflict and market review](us-israel-iran-conflict-market-scenarios-2026-08-19.md)
to 25 long and 25 short candidates for each of six horizons.

This is a research ranking, not a portfolio, personalized advice, or an
instruction to trade. Prices, probabilities, move ranges, borrow availability,
fund holdings, and geopolitical conditions must be refreshed before use.

## What changed

The first screen favored direct oil, gas, tanker, fertilizer, defense, travel,
and freight transmission. This version also tests:

- solar substitution through `TAN`;
- uranium miners, physical uranium, nuclear utilities, and nuclear services
  through `URNM`, `URA`, `NLR`, `CCJ`, `CEG`, and `BWXT`;
- helium scarcity and helium-saving equipment through `LIN`, `XOM`, and `GEHC`;
- cyber demand through `FTNT` and `PANW`;
- volatility and transaction volume through `CME` and `CBOE`;
- delayed fertilizer-to-food and farm-income transmission;
- rates, housing, discretionary consumption, airlines, cruises, and logistics;
- UAE and Dubai financial/property exposure through the U.S.-listed `UAE` ETF
  and separately identified Dubai-listed shares.

The horizons do not share a fixed list. Tanker and oil beta dominate days to
weeks. Solar, nuclear, cyber, industrial-gas, and equipment-substitution effects
need months for orders, contracts, policy, utilization, or estimate revisions.

## Discovery-led transmission graph

The expanded universe was built from the shock outward, not from a preset stock
list. The reviewed mechanisms were:

1. Lost crude, refined-product, LNG, LPG, fertilizer, sulfur, helium,
  petrochemical, and aluminum flows.
2. Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb ton-mile, vessel, port, salvage, insurance, and
  reinsurance effects.
3. Immediate fuel substitution into coal, nuclear dispatch, furnace oil, and
  emergency inventories.
4. Long-run substitution into nuclear, renewables, electric trucks, grid
  equipment, storage, and energy efficiency.
5. North American ethane/gas feedstock advantage versus naphtha-dependent Asian
  and European chemicals.
6. Plastic-to-paper packaging substitution and the offsetting risk of broad
  demand destruction.
7. Gulf water, electricity, desalination, port, pipeline, and strategic-reserve
  hardening.
8. State-backed cyber activity against energy, water, government, telecom, and
  industrial-control systems.
9. Subsea-cable disruption, repair-vessel and insurance constraints, and
  network redundancy. Satellite was rejected as a scalable replacement because
  Reuters' cited experts said it cannot carry comparable traffic.
10. Missile defense, drones, shipbuilding, munitions replenishment, and the
   distinction between an attack headline and a funded order.
11. Commodity, rates, and options hedging volume at exchanges.
12. Higher fuel, freight, food, plastic, metal, insurance, and borrowing costs
   for consumers and low-pass-through businesses.
13. U.S. midterm-driven reserve releases, subsidies, taxes, sanctions waivers,
   price caps, and other policy reversals that can hurt apparent winners.
14. Gulf trade finance, oil liquidity, capital inflow, physical attack, and
   capital-flight effects on UAE assets.
15. Post-ceasefire reconstruction of fields, refineries, LNG plants, ports,
   grids, water systems, and shipping lanes.

No ticker was admitted merely because it could be narrated into one branch. A
rank requires a listed instrument, a plausible earnings or valuation bridge,
and a catalyst that fits the horizon.

## Score decomposition

Every ranked row shows `M/T/F/V/R`, five integer components from 0 to 5:

| Component | Weight | A score of 5 means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `M` mechanism | 30% | A verified and causally direct transmission path. |
| `T` timing | 25% | The catalyst and financial recognition fit the stated horizon. |
| `F` financial capture | 20% | Revenue, margin, volume, balance sheet, or downside exposure should transmit materially. |
| `V` valuation/setup | 15% | Favorable crowding, valuation, or price asymmetry for the proposed direction. |
| `R` robustness | 10% | The thesis survives more than one plausible conflict path. |

For shorts, a high `F` means weak cost pass-through, leverage, cyclicality, or
another mechanism that captures downside. A high `V` means favorable downside
asymmetry, not a high-quality company.

The component vector is explanatory rather than a claim that a precise optimizer
was executed. Rank also considers the stated directional probability, the
midpoint of the move range, correlation with higher-ranked names, liquidity,
and the severity of the first invalidator.

`P` is the subjective probability that the ticker moves in the proposed
direction by the horizon. `Move` is the plausible cumulative directional range
from the August 19 observation. Uncertainty in `P` is at least 5-10 percentage
points. The ranges are not option-implied intervals or price targets.

## Evidence and instrument boundaries

- Yahoo Finance chart responses supplied delayed August 19 market observations
  and instrument identity. They are not a valuation authority.
- [Invesco](https://www.invesco.com/us/financial-products/etfs/product-detail?audienceType=Investor&productId=ETF-TAN)
  reported that `TAN` held 36 global solar-industry securities on August 18,
  tracked the MAC Global Solar Energy Index, and charged 0.70%. It is a
  concentrated, non-diversified solar ETF, not a generic clean-energy fund.
- [Global X](https://www.globalxetfs.com/funds/ura) reported that `URA` held 57
  companies across uranium mining and nuclear components, with 60.7% energy,
  26.0% industrials, and 7.1% utilities exposure at July 31. Its reported beta
  to the S&P 500 was 2.42, so it is not a defensive utility proxy.
- [Sprott](https://sprottetfs.com/urnm-sprott-uranium-miners-etf/) reported that
  `URNM` held 25 positions, with 81.13% uranium-related equities and 18.87%
  physical uranium at July 31. It is the most commodity-sensitive and least
  diversified nuclear vehicle used here.
- [VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/uranium-nuclear-energy-etf-nlr/overview/)
  describes `NLR` as exposure to utilities, uranium miners, nuclear engineering,
  equipment, and services. It is the broadest nuclear-value-chain vehicle here.
- Cheniere's contract structure and corrected `LNG` thesis remain documented in
  the parent review. `LNG` is not treated as a pure spot-LNG instrument.
- Reuters reported that Qatar normally supplies close to one-third of global
  helium. That verifies the scarcity channel, but not a material earnings effect
  for every semiconductor or MRI company. Semiconductor names are therefore not
  ranked solely on helium.
- `LIN` is included as a diversified industrial-gas supplier, but helium is only
  one part of its business. `XOM` has helium exposure inside a much larger
  integrated energy company. Both receive lower mechanism scores than pure
  tanker, fertilizer, or uranium exposures.
- `GEHC` is a long-horizon equipment-substitution candidate, not an immediate
  helium-price trade. Its mechanism score is deliberately low.
- The supplied iShares product ID `239676` resolved to the iShares MSCI Poland
  ETF, not `UAE`. The live market-data response nevertheless identifies ticker
  `UAE` as the iShares MSCI UAE ETF. Because current issuer holdings were not
  verified in this review, `UAE` receives a low mechanism score and is not
  described as a pure Dubai-bank basket.
- Yahoo identified `EMIRATESNBD.AE`, `DIB.AE`, `DFM.AE`, and `EMAAR.AE` as
  Dubai-listed shares quoted in AED. They are not assumed to be shortable or
  accessible in a U.S. account. `FAB.AE` did not resolve and is excluded.

## Portfolio and execution limits

- These are independent rankings, not 25 simultaneous positions. Many rows are
  highly correlated expressions of one underlying shock.
- The screen does not optimize position size, beta, factor exposure, sector
  concentration, gross/net exposure, drawdown, or portfolio loss.
- Short borrow, recalls, squeeze risk, dividends, option volatility, slippage,
  and tax were not collected.
- A short probability above 50% does not make naked shorting appropriate. Losses
  can exceed the initial investment.
- Reference prices are delayed observations and were captured at different
  moments on August 19. They are not synchronized closes.
- The probability is for direction, while the move range is conditional and
  judgmental. They should not be multiplied as though they were a complete
  expected-return distribution.
- Company results can be dominated by unrelated earnings, regulation,
  litigation, financing, weather, management, or valuation events.
- ETFs add holdings, country, currency, methodology, fee, tracking, and
  concentration risk. Holdings change.
- Local Dubai shares add market access, settlement, currency, ownership,
  liquidity, short-sale, and disclosure constraints that were not validated.
- The weakest entries are retained to satisfy the requested top 25. They are
  clearly lower-confidence research candidates, not forced trades.

## Update procedure

1. Refresh the conflict probabilities in the parent note.
2. Verify actual Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb crossings, insurance, freight, and
   refinery/LNG/fertilizer flows.
3. Refresh all prices at one timestamp and obtain current borrow and option data.
4. Recheck every ETF's holdings and methodology from the issuer.
5. Recheck each company's hedges, contracts, pass-through, debt, geography,
   backlog, and next earnings date.
6. Re-score `M/T/F/V/R`; do not merely update prices.
7. Remove any row whose direction probability is 50% or lower.
8. Collapse correlated rows before constructing a portfolio.
9. Record every correction instead of silently rewriting the prior estimate.
